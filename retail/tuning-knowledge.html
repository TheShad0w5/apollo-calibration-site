01 / FLASHING TOOLS

What do I need to flash my Subaru?

A tune file needs a compatible tool to write it to the ECU. A generic Bluetooth code reader does not replace a supported flashing device. The right route depends on the model year, engine, ECU and existing tune.

For a COBB Accessport tune

  • A supported Accessport for your exact vehicle, with its OBD-II cable.
  • A computer, USB data cable and COBB’s Accessport Manager for transferring files and logs.
  • Stable vehicle battery support during programming.
  • The correct tune file for your car, installed parts and fuel.

Before buying a used device

Confirm its part number and vehicle support, plus whether it is unmarried and ready for another car. An Accessport pairs with one vehicle at a time; a low used price does not establish that it can be installed on yours.

Send the device details and your car’s information before ordering a tune.

The Accessport writes the file; Accessport Manager transfers files between the handheld and computer. If you already use another system, an ECU swap or a standalone, ask me to confirm the supported hardware, software and any license requirements first.

02 / STI & EJ FUELING DATA

Why does my STI need a wideband?

A wideband measures exhaust oxygen so fueling can be evaluated as air/fuel ratio (AFR) or lambda. For remote tuning, I need that measurement recorded alongside the other engine data. A gauge you can watch is useful, but a gauge reading alone is not a usable tuning log.

For my 2017-and-earlier WRX/STI custom remote fueling work, an external wideband and an approved recording method are required. For 2018+ STI applications, I review the measurement plan individually. The same advance planning applies to 2015–2017 FA20 WRX remote fueling work; later WRX requirements are reviewed for the specific setup.

On older EJ applications, the factory front oxygen sensor is not a substitute for the external measurement needed for this work. The added wideband does not mean removing the factory sensor required by the ECU.

The complete setup matters

Confirm the sensor, controller, installation and a compatible way to record its output. A harness alone is not a complete wideband system, and a dashboard video does not replace the requested log. If a remote logging solution does not fit your car, we can discuss an in-person dyno session.

03 / EQUIPMENT TO REVIEW

The iWire wideband kit:
check the logging route first.

iWire’s AEM X-Series wideband with gauge and plug-and-play harness ↗ is an option to review for a compatible Subaru. Select the exact vehicle fitment and follow iWire’s installation instructions.

Display compatibility is not Accessport logging compatibility.

iWire says the gauge kit’s standard configuration preserves the rear O2 sensor but does not send wideband data into the ECU. Its documentation also states that post–Green Speed Accessport software does not support its external ECU-input logging method.

Do not buy this kit expecting its harness alone to put AFR into a current Accessport log. Send me your year, device and proposed wideband first so we can confirm an approved recording method.

The gauge kit and the version without a gauge are different products. Confirm the complete equipment list and recording plan before purchasing either one. This product link is for equipment research; it is not a guarantee that the kit completes your remote-tuning setup.

04 / BEFORE FLASHING

Prepare the car and the power supply.

Follow the current instructions for your exact device and vehicle. For Accessport installation and uninstallation, COBB calls for a 10–20 amp battery charger rather than a tender or jump pack. Turn off accessories and resolve other devices that may interfere with ECU communication.

Do not unplug the device or interrupt power while programming. If something fails, keep the displayed error and contact the device manufacturer or me before improvising a recovery.

Record the current device, tune-file name and any fault codes before we start. Tell me about an unknown previous tune, engine or ECU swap, or recent dealer software update.

Follow the COBB installation instructions ↗ for the programming procedure.

05 / DATA THAT HELPS

Send logs I can use.

I will specify the channels and operating conditions for your car. Use the requested file format and identify the tune revision and fuel used. Keep the original log file; screenshots of gauges omit the time-based data needed for review.

A useful note might read: “Revision 2, 93 octane, hesitation during the requested light-throttle test, no hardware changes.” Report symptoms honestly and stop if the car develops a misfire, leak, warning light or other abnormal behavior.

Collect data only as instructed in appropriate, controlled conditions. You do not need to invent a full-throttle test to send the first inquiry.

06 / FUEL & THE FULL PARTS LIST

“Stage 2” is not a parts list.

Send the actual intake, MAF housing, turbo, boost-control, exhaust, injectors and pump details, plus engine internals when applicable. Package names vary between suppliers. A map for a different intake or fuel is not automatically appropriate for yours.

Choose fuel you can consistently obtain.

Tell me the pump grade and whether it is labelled AKI or RON, plus your region. If using ethanol, include the intended blend and how it will be measured. A station’s E85 label alone does not establish the ethanol content in your tank.

Flex fuel needs compatible hardware and software.

An ethanol-content sensor does not by itself create a flex-fuel calibration. The ECU strategy, sensing system and fuel-delivery hardware all need review. A legacy kit is not proof that the current tuning software supports it.

07 / SAVE THIS CHECKLIST

Ready for a tuning conversation?

  • Vehicle year, model, engine and transmission identified.
  • Current tuning device, ECU history and tune-file name recorded.
  • Complete installed and planned parts list prepared.
  • Fuel grade, region and ethanol plans specified.
  • Wideband and recording method confirmed where required.
  • Known faults, leaks, misfires and maintenance concerns disclosed.
  • Computer, cables and battery support ready for the chosen process.
  • Questions about the service resolved before ordering.

A tune does not repair a mechanical problem. I may request further checks before accepting the car for calibration. Do not change the hardware or fuel midway through revisions without discussing the scope first.
