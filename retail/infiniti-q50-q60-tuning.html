CONFIRM THE ENGINE FIRST

Q50 and Q60 are model names—not one tuning setup.

This page focuses on the 3.0-liter twin-turbo VR30DDTT. Include your year, trim, market and AWD or RWD configuration when you get in touch. If you have an earlier 3.7-liter car or another engine, send those details for a separate compatibility review.

EcuTek reprograms the supported factory ECU. Your exact ECU software, existing tune, equipment and license status determine the programming route; I confirm those details before payment.

BEFORE WE START

Send the details that shape the tune.

  • Year, model, trim, engine, transmission, market and AWD or RWD.
  • Complete modifications list, current tune and any known mechanical or diagnostic issue.
  • Fuel grade and intended ethanol use, including installed fuel hardware and sensing.
  • EcuTek equipment and license details, plus any existing logs.
  • Your goals and whether you want remote tuning or an arranged dyno session.

I confirm the log channels and test procedure for your setup. Useful areas include boost control, both banks’ fueling, fuel-pressure behavior, temperatures and ignition/knock feedback. Follow the instructions agreed for your car rather than copying another platform’s logging list.

Q50 / Q60 QUESTIONS

Clear answers before you book.

Is Red Sport tuning the same as a 3.0t tune?

The trim is part of the setup review, alongside the ECU, installed hardware and fuel. I confirm the calibration scope for your actual car rather than assume one file fits every Q50 or Q60.

What about flex fuel, map switching or transmission tuning?

Where supported, RaceROM features can be discussed with the appropriate software and hardware. Flex-fuel work also needs an agreed sensing and fueling strategy. Transmission tuning is a separate compatibility and scope check; these features are not automatically included in every quote.

Do I need to buy new EcuTek hardware?

Send what you already own first. I confirm the supported programming route and the equipment and licensing required for your ECU before you purchase anything.

Does Mary’s 405.1-hp result mean a five-horsepower gain over a factory rating?

No. A factory rating is not an untuned dyno pull from Mary’s car. A measured gain needs a comparable baseline and final test; the archive shown here documents her recorded configuration.

Can I book a 3.7-liter Q50 or Q60 through this page?

Send the year and engine details first. This VR30 page does not establish compatibility for a different engine or ECU.
