Tuning or calibration?

Tuning is what most enthusiasts call it. Calibration is the technical work behind the tune. When we’re talking about ECU tuning, the words often describe the same work: adjusting how your engine is controlled to suit its hardware, fuel and intended use.

Tuning

The broad term for adjusting a vehicle or engine to suit a goal. In an ECU tuning conversation, it usually means changing how the engine computer controls the engine.

Back to terms ↑

ECU calibration

The technical process of setting and adjusting the ECU’s tables, settings and control strategies for the engine, hardware and fuel. In this context, tuning and calibration often describe the same work—not two separate services.

Back to terms ↑

Custom tune

A tune developed and refined for an individual vehicle’s setup, fuel and goals. Custom tuning can be performed remotely or with the vehicle at a dyno.

Back to terms ↑

Pro tune / protune

Common enthusiast shorthand for a tune created by a professional tuner, usually a custom tune. COBB also uses Protuner terminology for its tuning network. The label alone does not tell you where the tuning happens or what is included.

Back to terms ↑

E-tune / etune / remote tune

A tune developed remotely through an agreed exchange of vehicle details, datalogs and revised tune files. The owner or local operator carries out the requested checks and logging. Explore supported COBB e-tuning.

Back to terms ↑

Dyno tune

Tuning carried out with the vehicle or engine on a dynamometer, which measures output under test conditions. The dyno provides a controlled setting for evaluating changes; a dyno power run alone is not necessarily a tuning session. Learn about dyno tuning.

Back to terms ↑

OTS map / off-the-shelf tune

A pre-developed tune intended for a specified combination of vehicle, hardware and fuel. It is not individually developed for your car. Read the provider’s exact map notes and compatibility requirements.

Back to terms ↑

Map / tune file

In everyday tuning talk, “map” can mean the tune file loaded into an ECU. Inside that file, individual maps or tables can describe how a setting changes with conditions such as engine speed and load.

Back to terms ↑

Base map

An initial configuration or starting tune used to begin setup and validation. It is not automatically a finished tune or approval to drive under every condition. Confirm its intended use with the tuner.

Back to terms ↑

ECU / engine management

The electronic control unit and associated system that use sensor information to control engine functions such as fuel delivery and ignition.

Back to terms ↑

Standalone ECU

An aftermarket engine-management computer used in place of the original engine controller for a particular installation. Emtron, Haltech, Link ECU and MoTeC are examples discussed at Calibrations LLC. Installation, configuration and tuning must suit the vehicle. Explore standalone ECU tuning.

Back to terms ↑

Datalog / log

A time-based recording of selected ECU channels, such as RPM, throttle position, temperatures and fueling data. A log helps the tuner understand what happened and when. Learn about datalog review.

Back to terms ↑

Tune revision

An updated tune file produced after reviewing data or changing the agreed setup. The number of revisions and what changes are covered depend on the tuning scope.

Back to terms ↑

Stage 1 / Stage 2

Package names used by a particular supplier. They are not a universal specification across brands or platforms. The exact parts list, fuel and map requirements matter more than the stage number.

Back to terms ↑

Which kind of tune do I need?

Custom or OTS describes how a tune is developed. Remote or dyno describes how the work is carried out. Factory ECU or standalone describes the engine-management hardware. Those categories can overlap.
