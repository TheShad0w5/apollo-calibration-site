THE INSTALLATION COMES FIRST

Plug-in convenience still needs a setup review.

Know the ECU generation

Identify the model, generation and firmware, and keep a copy of the current PCLink tune. The software and features must be matched to that system. A newer product photograph does not establish support for an older ECU’s functions.

Review the vehicle connections

Plug-in fitment addresses a particular application; engine swaps, repinned harnesses and added sensors can change that picture. For a wire-in installation, provide a pinout and the installer’s notes so we can review the actual connections.

Define the fuel strategy

For a flex-fuel project, include the ethanol sensor, fuel system, injectors and intended fuel range. The sensor reading is only one part of the system. We agree how the combination will be tested and which data is needed before relying on the finished strategy.

Where it fits

The starting point is the exact ECU model and its supported functions. Plug-in fitment still needs a review of the car’s wiring, sensors and modifications.

Software and logging

PCLink is the configuration, tuning and log-review software. Choose the release for the exact ECU generation; a G4+, G4X or newer system should not be identified only as “a Link.” Link maintains current and legacy software information.

Send the current tune and an original log with the ECU model, firmware and PCLink version. A log records what the engine did; the tune explains how the ECU was configured at the time.

Plug-in or wire-in?

A plug-in ECU is designed around a specific application’s connections. A wire-in ECU uses a separately built or adapted harness. Both still need the correct sensor setup, pin assignments and engine configuration, especially after hardware or engine swaps.

What to send Keith

  • ECU model and generation, firmware and calibration file.
  • Whether the installation is plug-in or wire-in.
  • Engine, trigger, fuel-system and throttle specifications, plus logs.

If you are unsure which system you have, start with a photo of the ECU label and the details you know.

Before your tuning session

Confirm the correct PCLink software and connection method for the ECU generation. Share the existing calibration and a short description of the issue or goal so I can plan the session.

Link ECU planning questions.

Can you tune an LS swap on Link?

My Link experience includes an LS1-swapped S14. Send the ECU generation, engine and trigger details, throttle arrangement, fuel system and wiring information so I can assess your specific conversion and plan the work.

Can you supply hardware as well as tune it?

I offer ECU sales and project packages. Send your engine, intended use and existing equipment so we can confirm the hardware, accessories and calibration scope before quoting. Product photos are examples, not promises of stock or fitment.

Can this be handled remotely?

That depends on the installation, the work needed and the available data. Send the project details first. An unresolved wiring or mechanical issue may require hands-on checks before calibration can progress.

Can I use a dyno I have arranged?

Yes, subject to coordination. Confirm the location, equipment, operator, schedule and scope with me before booking. Travel and in-person work require planning and are quoted for the project.

Plan the next step

Tell me about your car, the equipment already installed and what you want to improve. I can review the setup before you order a tune or buy more parts.
