MY APPROACH

Build the calibration around the car.

Identify the ECU and software first

Send the exact Haltech model, firmware and tuning-software version. Haltech publishes NSP and information about supported Elite upgrades; compatibility must be checked for your equipment. Do not update or convert an existing configuration just to prepare for an inquiry.

Plan the first start and the dyno visit

Before scheduling, identify who will verify wiring, sensor readings, fuel pressure and base timing on the vehicle. For remote work, we need an agreed way to communicate and collect usable data. A dyno session also needs a suitable facility and an operator.

First-start work, troubleshooting and a complete tune are different scopes. Send the current state of your project so I can identify the next useful step and quote the work accordingly.

What to send before we start.

  • Vehicle and engine: model, displacement, engine work, turbo or supercharger, transmission and intended use.
  • Management system: exact ECU, firmware/software, current calibration and known access restrictions. Do not send passwords.
  • Fuel and sensors: injectors, pumps, regulator, fuel type, wideband controller and available pressure or temperature sensors.
  • Current condition: whether it runs, known faults, wiring documentation and sample logs if available.
  • Working arrangement: your location, installer or dyno shop, available equipment and whether you need setup, diagnosis or a complete tune.

I confirm compatibility, measurement requirements, scope and pricing before payment. Standalone work is quoted individually; the standard Subaru $400 e-tune package does not automatically cover it.

HALTECH TUNING FAQ

Plan the work before buying parts.

Can you help with first-start setup?

First-start and configuration inquiries are welcome. I review the wiring, hardware information and project status before confirming the scope. A starting calibration is not a finished full-load tune.

Should I use NSP or ESP?

Provide the ECU model, firmware and software currently in use. Haltech’s downloads and model documentation are the reference for compatibility; I confirm the workflow before changes are made.

Can this be tuned remotely or at my local dyno shop?

Send your location, facility and equipment details. I assess whether remote work is appropriate or whether an in-person session is the better fit. Worldwide travel sessions are quoted around the project and facility; submitting an inquiry does not reserve a date.

What does a wideband contribute?

I need usable mixture data alongside the engine information requested for the project. Tell me how the wideband controller is connected and logged. An installed display alone does not confirm that the necessary data is available.

Does engine protection replace mechanical checks?

No. Any configured protection depends on suitable sensors, correct readings and the available ECU strategy. Mechanical condition and installation still need verification; a calibration cannot guarantee against failure.
