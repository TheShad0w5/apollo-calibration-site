Response matters as much as peak output.

I want to know how you use the car: daily driving, a weekend road car or competition. Transient throttle response, the relationship between pedal input and boost, and predictable power delivery shape the tuning plan.

Tell me about your available fuel, intake and exhaust parts, cooling changes, turbochargers and fuel-system hardware. Existing warning lights or drivability issues belong in that conversation too. A power target needs to fit the complete vehicle.

VR30 tuning questions.

Can you tune a stock car or a car with bolt-ons?

Yes, I review both. Send the exact parts, fuel and goals so I can confirm a suitable scope. A stock-turbo car and an upgraded-turbo build need different plans.

Do I need an EcuTek interface and license?

Tell me what you already have. I will confirm the required flashing and datalogging equipment and licensing for the agreed workflow. Existing equipment or a previous tune does not automatically cover every requirement.

Can we discuss ethanol, flex fuel or RaceROM features?

Yes. I assess the fuel system, sensors, ECU software and available features together. Flex fuel, map switching and transmission work are quoted only where supported and agreed; they are not automatically included with every engine tune.

How much power will my car make?

I do not promise a universal horsepower number. Fuel, hardware, mechanical condition, operating conditions and the measurement method all affect the result. We agree on goals around your actual setup.

How long do remote revisions take?

For approved remote map services, initial files and agreed revisions are usually delivered the same business day and within one business day after all required information, usable logs and applicable payment are received. Business hours are Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–6 p.m. Pacific. This is per delivery, not the duration of the complete tuning process.
